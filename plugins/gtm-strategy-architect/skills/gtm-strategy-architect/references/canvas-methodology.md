# GTM Canvas Methodology

## Purpose

The GTM Canvas is a decision system for designing and evaluating a go-to-market strategy. It is not a linear checklist. Work across blocks in the order that best resolves the current strategic uncertainty while preserving the canonical structure below.

For every block, capture: objective, key questions, relevant frameworks, decisions, expected output, evidence requirements, and a quality check.

## 1. Market Intelligence — Understand the landscape

**Objective:** Establish whether an attractive, reachable, and timely market opportunity exists.

**Scope and questions**
- Market size and growth: What are TAM, SAM, and realistic SOM? Can estimates be built bottom-up?
- Trends and dynamics: Which technology, customer, regulatory, economic, and behavioral shifts matter?
- Market maturity: Is the category emerging, growing, mature, or declining?
- Competitive landscape: What direct, indirect, and status-quo alternatives exist?
- Market timing: Why now? What makes adoption easier or harder?

**Frameworks:** TAM/SAM/SOM; top-down and bottom-up sizing; PESTEL; Five Forces; market lifecycle; competitor mapping; trend analysis.

**Decision points:** Attractive market? Structural tailwinds? Competitive whitespace? Timing to enter?

**Expected output:** Market definition, sizing range with assumptions, dynamics, competitor/alternative map, timing thesis.

**Evidence:** Dated sources, transparent sizing assumptions, primary customer evidence where possible. Distinguish market data from extrapolation.

**Quality check:** Market boundaries are explicit; sizing is not double-counted; estimates are not presented as audited facts.

## 2. Customer & Segmentation — Know who you're targeting

**Objective:** Identify customers with a meaningful problem, ability to buy, and reachable buying process.

**Scope and questions**
- ICP: Which firmographic, technographic, behavioral, and situational traits predict fit?
- Segments and personas: Which groups have materially different needs or buying contexts?
- JTBD: What progress are customers trying to make?
- Buying committee: Who initiates, evaluates, approves, pays, uses, and can block?
- Needs and willingness to pay: How costly is the problem and what evidence indicates budget?

**Frameworks:** ICP; segmentation; personas; JTBD; buying committee mapping; Voice of Customer; willingness-to-pay research.

**Decision points:** Which segment has strongest need, urgency, budget, and accessibility? Which customer is not a fit?

**Expected output:** Prioritized segments, ICP definition, jobs/pain points, buying roles, evidence-backed needs.

**Evidence:** Interviews, usage data, win/loss, CRM, support, behavioral data, pricing research. Label anecdotal evidence.

**Quality check:** ICP is actionable and excludes as well as includes; persona claims are not stereotypes.

## 3. Market Entry Strategy — Choose where and how to start

**Objective:** Select a focused entry point that creates a credible path to expansion.

**Scope and questions**
- Beachhead: Where can the product win first?
- Segment prioritization: Which segments combine attractiveness and ability to win?
- Use cases: Which problem/use-case pairing is urgent and demonstrable?
- Expansion roadmap: What adjacent segments, use cases, or geographies follow?

**Frameworks:** Beachhead strategy; attractiveness vs. ability-to-win; Ansoff Matrix; use-case prioritization.

**Decision points:** Initial segment, first use case, entry geography/channel, expansion sequence.

**Expected output:** Beachhead rationale, rejected alternatives, entry milestones, expansion hypotheses.

**Evidence:** Segment economics, customer access, competitive intensity, product fit, sales-cycle evidence.

**Quality check:** Entry scope is narrow enough to focus resources and broad enough to support a viable opportunity.

## 4. Product Readiness — Ensure the product is ready

**Objective:** Determine whether the product can deliver the promised outcome to the chosen segment.

**Scope and questions**
- Product-market fit evidence: What demonstrates repeatable value, retention, and demand?
- Key product gaps: What prevents adoption, value realization, or differentiation?
- Integrations and ecosystem: Which dependencies are required?
- Onboarding and activation: How quickly and reliably do customers reach value?

**Frameworks:** PMF evidence assessment; Sean Ellis test where appropriate; feature-gap analysis; activation analysis; RICE.

**Decision points:** Ready to launch, launch with constraints, or delay? Which gaps are critical vs. deferrable?

**Expected output:** Readiness assessment, critical gaps, mitigations, activation path.

**Evidence:** Product analytics, retention/cohorts, support tickets, customer feedback, implementation data.

**Quality check:** Product claims match actual capability; PMF signals are interpreted in context, not reduced to one metric.

## 5. Positioning & Differentiation — Own a clear place

**Objective:** Make the product's value and distinctiveness relevant and credible to the target customer.

**Scope and questions**
- Competitive alternatives: What do customers use or do today, including doing nothing?
- Category positioning: What market/category frame helps customers understand the offer?
- Value proposition: What outcome is delivered, for whom, and why is it better?
- Key messaging: What proof and messages support the promise?

**Frameworks:** Competitive alternatives; April Dunford's Obviously Awesome; Value Proposition Canvas; UVP; messaging hierarchy; perceptual mapping.

**Decision points:** Target audience, category frame, differentiated value, proof points, message hierarchy.

**Expected output:** Positioning statement, value proposition, differentiation pillars, message map, proof requirements.

**Evidence:** Customer language, win/loss, alternatives analysis, product proof, competitive research.

**Quality check:** Positioning is customer-relevant, differentiated, specific, and supportable by product evidence.

## 6. Business & Monetization Model — Capture value sustainably

**Objective:** Select how value is packaged, priced, sold, and captured.

**Scope and questions**
- Revenue model: Subscription, usage-based, transaction, services, licensing, or hybrid?
- Pricing strategy: What anchors price to customer value and willingness to pay?
- Packaging and tiers: Which capabilities/outcomes belong in each tier?
- Value metric: What unit scales with customer value?
- Unit economics: Can gross margin, CAC, retention, and payback support the model?

**Frameworks:** Business Model Canvas; value-based, competitor-based, and cost-plus pricing; Good-Better-Best; Van Westendorp; Gabor-Granger; value metric design; unit economics.

**Decision points:** Revenue model, price metric, price architecture, packaging boundaries, discounting principles.

**Expected output:** Monetization hypothesis, package architecture, pricing rationale, economics assumptions and tests.

**Evidence:** WTP research, customer budgets, competitor pricing, cost-to-serve, usage/value data, conversion and retention.

**Quality check:** Price metric tracks delivered value; packaging supports segmentation; financial assumptions are explicit.

## 7. GTM Motion & Distribution — Reach and convert customers

**Objective:** Choose a route to market that matches customer buying behavior and economics.

**Scope and questions**
- GTM motion: PLG, sales-led, partner-led, community-led, or hybrid?
- Channels: Organic, paid, outbound, inbound, marketplaces, communities, partners?
- Sales process: What stages, roles, proof, and cycle length are required?
- Key partnerships: Which partners provide access, trust, integration, or delivery?

**Frameworks:** PLG vs. SLG; AARRR; funnel analysis; channel prioritization; partner mapping.

**Decision points:** Primary motion, channel mix, handoffs, qualification, partner strategy.

**Expected output:** GTM motion design, channel priorities, funnel stages, sales/partner process.

**Evidence:** CAC and conversion by channel, sales-cycle data, customer preferences, channel reach and intent.

**Quality check:** Motion fits buying complexity, ACV, urgency, onboarding needs, and available resources.

## 8. Launch & Enablement — Execute with confidence

**Objective:** Coordinate market introduction and ensure teams can deliver the promise.

**Scope and questions**
- Launch strategy and timeline: What is the launch tier, audience, sequence, and milestone plan?
- Marketing campaigns: What demand and adoption activities support the launch?
- Sales enablement: What assets, training, qualification, and objection handling are needed?
- Customer success readiness: Can customers onboard, adopt, and realize value?

**Frameworks:** Launch tiering; launch roadmap; RACI; integrated marketing communications; sales enablement; CS readiness.

**Decision points:** Launch scope/date, launch audience, readiness gates, enablement owners.

**Expected output:** Launch plan, campaign plan, enablement checklist, operational readiness gates.

**Evidence:** Product readiness, message validation, channel plans, support capacity, sales feedback.

**Quality check:** No launch promise exceeds product or operational capability; owners and dependencies are clear.

## 9. Execution & Economics — Turn strategy into results

**Objective:** Ensure the strategy is resourced, measurable, and economically plausible.

**Scope and questions**
- Budget and resources: What people, tools, spend, and time are needed?
- Team and ownership: Who is accountable for outcomes and dependencies?
- Funnel metrics: What are conversion rates, CAC, and bottlenecks?
- Revenue targets and payback: What targets are plausible under stated assumptions?

**Frameworks:** Resource planning; RACI; funnel modelling; CAC/LTV; payback; revenue forecasting; scenarios and sensitivity analysis.

**Decision points:** Resource allocation, targets, capacity constraints, investment gates.

**Expected output:** Resourcing plan, ownership map, funnel model, base/upside/downside scenarios.

**Evidence:** Historical performance, benchmarks with context, cost data, capacity, pipeline and retention.

**Quality check:** Targets reconcile with funnel math, sales capacity, budget, and delivery constraints.

## 10. Validation & Optimization — Learn and iterate

**Objective:** Reduce the uncertainty that could invalidate the strategy.

**Scope and questions**
- Key assumptions: Which beliefs are both uncertain and consequential?
- Experiments and tests: What is the cheapest credible test?
- Success criteria: What observable result changes the decision?
- Iteration plan: What will be changed, by whom, and when?

**Frameworks:** Hypothesis-driven experimentation; assumption mapping; Riskiest Assumption Test; ICE/RICE; Build-Measure-Learn; A/B testing where valid.

**Decision points:** Test priority, method, threshold, duration, decision rule.

**Expected output:** Ranked assumptions, experiment backlog, test cards, decision rules, learning log.

**Evidence:** Experiment design, baseline, sample/limitations, observed results.

**Quality check:** Tests measure the assumption, not a vanity proxy; thresholds and next actions are set before results.

## 11. Risks & Considerations — Plan for uncertainty

**Objective:** Identify threats and dependencies that could materially change the strategy.

**Scope and questions**
- Market risks: Demand, timing, category, concentration, or macro shifts?
- Competitive risks: Incumbent response, substitutes, pricing pressure?
- Execution risks: Capability, talent, capacity, partners, delivery?
- Regulatory/external factors: Legal, privacy, security, geopolitical, platform, or policy constraints?

**Frameworks:** SWOT; PESTEL; probability-impact matrix; pre-mortem; scenario planning; mitigation planning.

**Decision points:** Which risks are acceptable, mitigable, or strategy-blocking? What triggers a change?

**Expected output:** Risk register, severity, owner, mitigation, trigger, contingency.

**Evidence:** Regulatory sources, operational data, expert input, scenario assumptions.

**Quality check:** High-impact risks have owners and response plans; external constraints are not treated as generic boilerplate.

## 12. Key Metrics — Measure what matters

**Objective:** Connect strategic outcomes to leading indicators and operational metrics.

**Scope and questions**
- Acquisition: Are the right prospects reached efficiently?
- Activation: Do customers reach the first meaningful value?
- Retention: Do they continue to receive value?
- Revenue: Does monetization grow sustainably?
- Unit economics: Is growth economically viable?

**Frameworks:** AARRR; North Star; KPI trees; funnel metrics; cohort analysis; retention; revenue metrics; unit economics.

**Decision points:** North Star and input metrics, metric definitions, owners, cadence, thresholds.

**Expected output:** KPI tree, metric dictionary, dashboard requirements, review cadence, alert thresholds.

**Evidence:** Instrumentation, consistent definitions, reliable data sources, cohort and segment cuts.

**Quality check:** Metrics map to strategic outcomes, have clear formulas and owners, and avoid rewarding local optimization at the expense of the whole system.

## Cross-block dependencies

The typical dependency chain is:

Opportunity → Customer → Entry Point → Product Fit → Positioning → Business Model → Route to Market → Launch → Execution → Validation → Risk Controls → Metrics.

This is a guide, not a mandatory linear sequence. A specific problem may start anywhere, but downstream and upstream implications must be revisited.

## Completion rule

A block is not complete because text exists in it. It is reviewable when its key decisions, rationale, evidence status, unknowns, dependencies, and next actions are visible. The overall strategy is only Evidence-Backed when critical decisions have adequate evidence and material contradictions have been resolved.
